Encoders And Decoders
---------------------------------------------------

Encoders basically transform one form of data into another, Most of the time it projects data into smaller spaces called latent space in Autoencoders. So you could have an image of 28*28 dimension which is 784 features for 1 input and convert this to 2D latent space which is just 2 dimension .
i.e (x1,x2,x3,....x784) ---> (x1,x2)

For more details use higher number of latent dimension .
in this case lets say just 2 dimension and we have dataset of pants and shirts then images closer to positive x could be a shirt , images more towards positive y could be a pant. 

Decoder ( in Autoencoder and VAE ) - Job of decoder is to reconstruct the image as best as it could from the latent space (input is latent space) and output is reconstructed image .

Since the latent space dimension is much smaller than original dimension , this acts as a bottleneck and hence using this we can create a new image (because some of details gets lost due to bottleneck )

how to create new image -->
Lets say we have dataset of peoples faces , with latent dimension = 10 .

Lets say latent dimension 1 (which represents x axis ) is learned such a way that positive x is for blonde haired people and negative for black haired.

So if we use encoder on one of the original images , get their latent space and move a little to the x , your image will have blonde hair .


A little more details about encoder and decoder 
--------------------------------------------------

So in practice we use encoder with Conv2D first so we can find edges and other things. Because if we directly connect a neural circuit to an image the result would not contain the structural relation of the image (since it would be flattened to 784 ). So we use multiple Conv2D layers to find out different patterns etc. Then we add neural circuitry on top (could add Layernorm etc too  ) 

And on Decoder side we use Conv2DTranspose to increase the dimension to original dimension. 


Problems with AutoEncoder
--------------------------------------------------

AutoEncoder is deterministic in nature , Once the model is trained the same input will always point to same location. And output will be the same too . 
Additionally AutoEncoder might create holes , e.g lets say an image latent space is (10,10) but area around it (lets say 10.5,9.5) might not give any readable results (total noise image).
SO we want an image to be pointed to a latent dimension in such a way that it gets spread (like a gaussian is spread around mean ).

Training AutoEncoder 
-------------------------------------------------

P_theta(x) represent probability of image dataset.


Let Z be the latent distribution 
So G(z) is the distribution of latent dimension Z .

Encoder Job is to predict G(z|x)

And Decoder job is to Find out P(x|z)

So we want to minimize   (Decoder(Encoder(P_theta(x)))-P_theta(x)) . 
We can use MSE mean square error since we want it to spread in all directions. 

so 
Loss= MSE ( (Decoder(Encoder(P_theta(x)))-P_theta(x)) ) 




Variational autoencoder 
-------------------------------------------------

SO we want continous probability distribution in latent space , and also we want image generation to be probabilistic not deterministic so we modify loss and Encoder

Here Encoder instead of outputting the image representation in latent space outputs the mean and variance of latent space outputs for the input

i.e  mean,Variance=Encoder(x) 

We want it to be spread equally so we use gassian.

Then we sample from this distribution using gaussian distribution with mean and variance that we found from encoder outputs. But since it is sampled , gradients cant flow back (cant flow back through randomize functions )

So we use reparatemirization trick .

We sample from a Gaussian dist of mean = 1 and std dev=1 given by epsilon

Then we generate our sample as 

x'=mean + sqrt(variance)*epsilon

Now using this we can assume epsilon to be a constant and hence gradients can flow back now. 

Notice how for same image , the latent space output is now different. 
This is the input to decoder.

Change in LOSS function 
------------------------

The loss function remains mostly similar to AutoEncoder but we want our Distribution to be more like Gaussian so we add additional penalty to it .

Loss= MSE ( (Decoder(Encoder(P_theta(x)))-P_theta(x)) ) + KL divergence of ( P_theta(z|x) || NOrmal(0,1) )

Since we want our distribution of latent space to be as close to Normal Distribution with 0 mean and 1 variance  as possible .

For KL divergence of P||Q and both are gaussiaan has a closed form solution which is 
KL = log ( std2/std1) + ( std1^2 + ( mean1-mean2  )^2)/(2*std2^2) -1/2 


Why ? Why do we force distriubtion to normal distributino with mean =0 and std=1 ??
ANswer is we need to force it in a closed space . if our mean outputs 300 or something that we have never seen , we will get noisy outputs . So we need to force it in a space that we want all outputs to be in .

KL divergence comapred to Normal with mean = 0 and stddev=1 isnt magical , we can find it with any mean and std dev (small enouggh ) , But you would have to make changes to reparameteization too .

it is completely fine to find KL divergence of (Encoder(X)|| Normal (mean = 0 , stddev=5)) . Here the spread would be wider though.


Finally we dont want KL divergence to have higher weight to reconstruction loss so we use something called Beta annealing. ( slowly grow weight of KL divergence term in loss ) 


Loss= MSE ( (Decoder(Encoder(P_theta(x)))-P_theta(x)) ) + Beta*KL divergence of ( P_theta(z|x) || NOrmal(0,1) )


in my trial i first reduced reconstruction term to a very small value (by epoch 5 )and then turned Beta = 0.1 to 0.5 at epoch 20.
